Eagle Mountain City Council, 19 November 2024

4 motions · 5 ordinance lines · 4 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Small Business Saturday declared for Nov. 30

The council proclaimed November 30, 2024 as Small Business Saturday in Eagle Mountain.

What it means: A ceremonial nod to local shops. No rule, fee, or program comes with it.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to approve a Proclamation of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Proclaiming November 30, 2024 as Small Business Saturday. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Consent agenda approved in one vote

The council passed its consent agenda, the batch of items handled together without separate discussion.

What it means: The record here does not list which items were in the batch, so nothing can be said about who they touch.

passed with a unanimous vote.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the consent agenda. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

10% of new homes in some zones must be affordable

The city now requires at least 10% of new homes in certain residential zones to be priced for households earning 80% or less of Utah County's median income.

What it means: If you build or buy in these zones, a share of the homes carry a recorded deed restriction: the owner must live there and the price is capped. Applies to new development, not existing homes.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.25 Residential Zones with the following changes: 1. EMMC 17.25.020(E)(2)(a)(iv) and 17.25.020(F)(1)(a)(iv) are changed to, “Deed Restrictions: A deed restriction shall be recorded for the property dedicated as a moderate income dwelling unit that establishes the unit shall be occupied by the owner and priced for a household with a gross income equal to or less than 80% of the median gross income for Utah County;” and 2. EMMC 17.25.020(E)(2)(a)(i) and 17.25.020(F)(1)(a)(i) are changed to, “Required Amount of Moderate Income Housing: A minimum of 10% of new residential development in this zone shall be dedicated to moderate income housing (MIH) units priced for occupancy by households with a gross income equal to or less than 80% of the median gross income for Utah County per Utah Code 10-9a-103.” Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Add transit grants to the state lobbying list

The council added a study of public transit innovation grants to the city's Legislative Priorities List for the state legislature.

What it means: Nothing changes on the ground. It tells Eagle Mountain's lobbyists what to raise at the Capitol, and any transit money would be years and several votes away.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved that a study of public transit innovation grants be added to the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Consent items approved, mortgage contract flagged

The council approved its consent agenda, pulling the credit card policy out and requiring the mortgage assistance contract to return to council if applications exceed 100.

What it means: Nothing changes day to day. If the city's mortgage assistance program draws more than 100 loan applications, the contract with its administrator comes back to the council for another look.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve the Consent Agenda, removing Item 13.A. - A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending Section 5.40 (Employee Credit Card Policy) of the Eagle Mountain City Employee Policies and Procedures Manual; and, including language in Item 13.B - Contract for Mortgage Assistance Program Administration with Ultimate Home Lending, LLC to state that the contract will be brought back to the City Council if loan applications exceed 100. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

New rules for keeping animals on your property

The city set standards for alternative animal management plans and tied the permit to the applicant, so it ends automatically when the property is sold.

What it means: If you keep animals under one of these plans, the approval is yours, not your land's. It does not transfer to a buyer — when you sell, the new owner has to apply on their own.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Title 6 and Title 17 to Establish Standards for Alternative Animal Management Plans and Update Other Zoning Provisions with the following addition: • The non-transferable Notice of Intent (NOI) is tied to the applicant rather than the property; therefore, the Animal Management Plan automatically terminates upon sale of the property. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Update the map of land the city could annex

The council updated its annexation policy plan, extending Area 1 to Utah Lake and requiring the map to mark old boundaries separately from newly expanded ones.

What it means: Nothing changes inside the city today. This matters to owners of unincorporated land on the edges — the council deliberately kept overlaps with neighboring cities so an owner can choose which city to join.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain City Annexation Policy Declaration as Part of the General Plan with the following conditions: 1. That the plan include the “Statement Addressing Comments of Affected Entities” as drafted by the City Attorney with the amendment that the SITLA and Saratoga Springs verbiage be omitted for the following reasons: a. The alignment in Area 6 matches the previous alignment from the Eagle Mountain City 2009 Annexation Policy Plan, and b. The annexation policy plan may overlap the plans from another jurisdiction, which would allow a property owner to have the option of which municipality would be most beneficial for annexation. 2. That the Annexation Policy Plan show Area 1 extending to Utah Lake, as presented, and 3. That the Annexation Policy Map show dates of previous versions of the annexation map/year it was approved, and 4. That the Annexation Policy Map show crosshatching for old property vs. newly expanded property boundaries. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

New recreation, arts, and parks tax created

The city wrote a new Recreation, Arts, and Park Tax into its municipal code, adding a chapter that establishes the tax.

What it means: This puts the tax on the books. The motion itself doesn't state a rate or a start date, so check the ordinance for what you would actually pay and when.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Enacting Chapter 3.45, "Recreation, Arts, and Park Tax", of the Eagle Mountain City Municipal Code. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

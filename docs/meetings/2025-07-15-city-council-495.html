Eagle Mountain City Council, 15 July 2025

11 motions · 23 ordinance lines · 10 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved

The council approved its consent agenda, the batch of items passed together without separate discussion.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is the routine bundle of items the council clears in one vote.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the Consent Agenda. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

City to dispose of 2.15 acres of land

The council approved disposing of 2.15 acres of city-owned land at Utah County parcel 59:044:0015 and set the terms.

What it means: This land leaves city hands. The motion does not say who receives it or what will go there, so there is nothing concrete yet for neighbors to plan around.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Disposal of 2.15 Acres of Designated "Significant Real Property" (Utah County Parcel #59:044:0015) Pursuant to EMMC 3.30.060 and 3.30.070, and Establishing Terms of the Disposal. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Revised plan for Town Center marketplace

The council approved an amended preliminary plat for the Marketplace at Eagle Mountain Town Center.

What it means: This is an early planning step for the Town Center marketplace, laying out how the land is divided. Nothing gets built on this approval alone.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt an Amended Preliminary Plat for Marketplace at Eagle Mountain Town Center. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Pay schedule for city officers updated

The council amended the compensation schedule for the city's statutory officers.

What it means: This sets what the city's statutory officers are paid. The motion does not state the new figures, and nothing changes for residents day to day.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah amending Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 2.20.020, Compensation Schedule of Statutory Officers. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Shared parking requirement lowered to 35%

The council changed the rules on where parking areas can go and cut the required shared parking from 40% to 35%.

What it means: This mainly touches developers and businesses building new commercial projects, which can now share slightly less of their parking. You may see lots laid out differently in future developments.

passed with a 3:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamNo
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 17.55.040(E) Location of Parking Areas; and Section 17.55.100 Shared Parking and Curb Cuts with the change in 17.55.100 from 40% required shared parking to 35% required shared parking. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Drive-thru lane rules put on hold

The council delayed a decision on changes to the city's rules for drive-thru lanes.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents or businesses — the existing drive-thru rules stay in place until the council takes the item back up.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.35.050 Drive-Thru Lanes. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Building standards for homes updated

The council changed the city code's definition of a "structure" and its residential development standards table.

What it means: If you are planning to build on your lot, the rules for what counts as a structure and the standards your project must meet have shifted. The motion itself doesn't spell out the specifics.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Title 17, Sections 17.10.030 Structure Definition, and 17.25.040 Residential Development Standards Table. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Up to 20.35 acres near Bypass Road to be traded

The council approved disposing of up to 20.35 acres of city-owned land known as the Bypass Road Property, to be exchanged for land chosen later.

What it means: The city is clearing the way to swap this land, but has not yet said what it will get in return. Staff must come back to the council before any trade is final.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Disposal of up to 20.35 acres of Public Real Property known as the Bypass Road Property and instruct Staff to return to City Council when the future property to be exchanged is determined for Council approval. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Sign rules now cover city fields and Rodeo Grounds

The council rewrote the city's sign rules and defined an "athletic facility" as a high school, middle school, or city-owned regulation field, or the Rodeo Grounds.

What it means: The sign rules that apply at athletic venues now reach city-owned fields and the Rodeo Grounds, not just school fields. For most residents, day-to-day life is unchanged.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.80 Sign Regulations and Sign Permits with the change in definition stating that an athletic facility is defined as a high school or middle school regulation size soccer, football, baseball or softball field. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.. Councilmembers requested an amended motion to include the Rodeo Grounds and City owned property in the proposed amendment. AMENDED MOTION: Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.80 Sign Regulations and Sign Permits with a change stating that an athletic facility is defined as a high school, middle school or City owned regulation size soccer, football, baseball, or softball field, or the Rodeo Grounds. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Zayo fiber franchise agreement put on hold

The council delayed a decision on a franchise agreement with Zayo Group, LLC, a fiber network company.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents right now — the agreement was set aside and will come back to the council another night.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

  • Rich WoodExcused

Lone Tree road funding added to priorities list

The council added a funding plan for roads serving the Lone Tree and Oquirrh Mountain Ranch areas through Airport Road to its legislative priorities list.

What it means: Nothing changes on the ground yet. This puts the road funding on the city's official ask list; any actual money and construction would still have to be approved later.

passed with a 4:0 vote.

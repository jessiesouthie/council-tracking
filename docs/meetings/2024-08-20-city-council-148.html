Eagle Mountain City Council, 20 August 2024

7 motions · 10 ordinance lines · 8 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

September named PTA Membership Month

The city proclaimed September 2024 as Parent Teacher Association Membership Month.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is a ceremonial recognition of local PTA volunteers, with no cost or requirement attached.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt a Proclamation of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Proclaiming September 2024 as Parent Teacher Association Membership Month, as read by Mayor Westmoreland. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Consent agenda approved, manager contract pulled

The council approved its consent agenda, but pulled out the interim city manager agreement with Stephen Mumford and shortened a legislative consulting renewal to two years.

What it means: This is routine housekeeping — nothing changes for residents. The interim city manager contract was set aside to be handled separately.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to approve the Consent Agenda, removing item 13.C. A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving an Agreement with Stephen Mumford for Interim City Manager Services, and amending item 10.A. Agreement Renewal – Legislative Executive Consulting, LLC to a two-year extension. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Decision delayed on Towns at Brylee Farms

The council delayed a decision on the development agreement for the Towns at Brylee Farms project.

What it means: Nothing changes for now — the project is on hold until the council takes it up again.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, approving the Towns at Brylee Farms Development Agreement. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

672-acre development deal near the data centers

The council took up a development agreement covering about 672 acres on two parcels, one near the Meta, Google, and QTS sites and one beside the Firefly area.

What it means: This touches landowners and neighbors near the data center campuses and in the southwest near Firefly. The council rewrote the buffering language so it only binds land once that land is annexed into the city.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Tract Development Agreement with the following change: 1. The last three sentences in Section 4.12 are removed and replaced with, “If Additional Property is annexed into this Agreement pursuant to this Section 4.12, then such future property shall be buffered as required by the Applicable Rules. This paragraph does not apply to any property located outside the City limits unless it is annexed into the City.” Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

Triple Tail redevelopment plan adopted

The city adopted the Triple Tail Community Reinvestment Project Area Plan, covering 24 parcels, to steer that land toward new development and economic uses.

What it means: If you live near 4000 North and Pole Canyon Boulevard east of Tyson Parkway, or south of 1000 North east of Pony Express Parkway, expect the city to actively pursue development on the land around you.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting the Triple Tail Community Reinvestment Project Area Plan, as Approved by the Redevelopment Agency of Eagle Mountain City, as the Official Community Reinvestment Project Area Plan for the Project Area and Directing that Notice of the Adoption be Given as Required by Statute. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

MHTN Architects hired to plan a downtown

The city hired MHTN Architects to create the Downtown Master Plan, choosing the firm after hearing pitches from three competing firms.

What it means: Nothing changes on the ground yet. This hires the firm that will draft the city's vision for a downtown, work that would shape what eventually gets built there.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving an Agreement for Consulting Services with MHTN Architects for the Downtown Master Plan. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

City drops ADU impact-fee item from lobbying list

The city removed the item on impact fees for accessory dwelling units from the list of priorities it asks the state legislature to act on.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents or for what you pay to build an ADU — the city simply stopped asking the legislature to take up the issue this session.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to remove the “MIHP regarding Impact Fees for ADUs” item from the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

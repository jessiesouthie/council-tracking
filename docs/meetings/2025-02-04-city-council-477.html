Four items pulled off the consent list

The council approved its consent agenda but pulled four items out of it for open discussion later in the meeting.

What it means: Those four items got debated in public rather than passing in a single silent vote. The step itself reaches no resident directly.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Firefly water tank change order put off

The council set aside a second change order on the Firefly 4 million gallon water tank without deciding it.

What it means: The tank work waits on a later meeting for this piece. Nothing about your water service changes in the meantime.

QTS development agreement amended

The council approved the first amendment to the city's development agreement with QTS; the motion doesn't say what the amendment changes.

What it means: A development agreement sets what a company may build and what it owes the city, so changes here can matter. The record doesn't say what was changed.

City buys 21 acres for a future civic campus

The city agreed to buy 20.99 acres of land from Ivory Homes and SITLA for a future civic campus.

What it means: The city now has land set aside for future civic buildings. Nothing is built yet: the motion doesn't say what will go there, what it cost, or when.

New water rights rules for new development

The council changed the city's development code to alter how water rights and early-stage concept plans are handled, with amendments made during the meeting.

What it means: Landowners and builders bringing new projects forward will have to meet the revised water rights requirements. If you are not proposing a development, nothing changes for you.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 16 Related to the Water Rights and Concept Plans with changes as stated. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Four zoning code sections updated

The council amended four sections of the city's zoning code; the motion gives no detail on what the changes actually do.

What it means: We can't tell you what changed: the record names only section numbers. They sit in Title 17, the zoning rules that govern what can be built and where.

passed with a unanimous 4:0 vote.

New standards for RV and trailer storage

The council rewrote the city's rules for outdoor storage of recreational vehicles and trailers, changing the standards that apply to that use.

What it means: This touches properties used to store RVs and trailers outdoors: storage yard owners and operators more than ordinary homeowners. The record doesn't spell out the new requirements, so check the updated chapter before relying on the old rules.

passed with a unanimous 4:0 vote.

Eagle Mountain City Council, 16 December 2025

8 motions · 8 ordinance lines · 5 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved, two items pulled

The council approved its consent agenda after pulling items 12.A. and 13.A. out for separate discussion.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is the routine step that passes uncontroversial items in one vote, with two set aside for their own debate.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve the Consent Agenda removing items 12.A. and 13.A. to be discussed during the regularly scheduled items. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Firefly NPA 9.A site plan approved

The council approved the master site plan for Firefly NPA 9.A, the layout that governs how that part of the Firefly area gets built.

What it means: This clears the way for building in that section of Firefly. The motion does not state what is going in or how much, so the details sit in the site plan itself.

passed with a 3:0 vote.

  • Brett WrightAbstain
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayAbstain
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to approve the Master Site Plan for Firefly NPA 9.A. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Decision delayed on SITLA land swap in Overland

The council delayed a decision on a land exchange agreement with the state land trust, SITLA, for properties in the Overland area.

What it means: No land changed hands and nothing changes for residents right now — the swap comes back to the council at a later meeting.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to table a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving a Land Exchange Agreement with SITLA for the Exchange of Properties in the Overland Area. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Decision delayed on alternative energy zone rules

The council delayed a decision on creating a new Alternative Energy Overlay Zone in the city code.

What it means: No rules changed. The proposal comes back to the council another day, so there is still time to weigh in before anything is decided.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes

  • Melissa ClarkYes

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Creating Chapter 17.64, Alternative Energy Overlay Zone, in the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Final zoning set for the White Hills area

The council assigned final zoning across the White Hills area, including one commercial lot, two agricultural parcels, and ten parcels set to rural agriculture.

What it means: If you own land in White Hills, this fixes what you may build there — most of it stays rural or farm land, with one parcel opened to neighborhood-scale commercial use.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Official Zoning Map and Future Land Use Map to Assign Final Zoning Designations to the White Hills Area with the following amendments: • Parcel 59:049:0042 (Owned by Bill Willson) be rezoned to Commercial Neighborhood (CN); and • Parcel 43:282:0001 and 43:282:0002 (Subdivision: J&J Ranches) be rezoned to Agriculture (AR); and • Per Planning Commission recommendation, the following Parcels be rezoned to Rural Agriculture 2 (RA2): 55:320:001, 55:320:002, 55:320:0013, 55:320:0005, 55:320:0006, 55:320:0007, 55:320:0008, 55:320:0009, 55:320:0010 and 55:320:0011. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

"Newspaper" update to subdivision and zoning code

The council amended two chapters of city code — 16.05 on subdivisions and 17.05 on zoning — in what the city calls the Newspaper Amendment.

What it means: The motion does not say what the amendment actually changes, so there is nothing concrete residents will see or do differently based on this text alone.

passed with a 4:1 vote.

Pacific Springs development agreement approved

The council approved a development agreement for Pacific Springs, the contract setting the terms under which that project can be built.

What it means: The motion itself does not spell out the size, location, or number of homes, so the specifics are in the agreement rather than the vote. It clears the way for the Pacific Springs project to move forward.

passed with a 4:1 vote.

Ask the state to fund a sidewalk near Walmart

The council added construction of a sidewalk on SR-73 and Spring Run near Walmart to the list of priorities it takes to state lawmakers.

What it means: Nothing is built or funded yet — this only puts the sidewalk on the city's ask list. If the state pays for it, people walking near Walmart get a sidewalk.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to include a budget adjustment for construction costs for a sidewalk on SR-73 and Spring Run near Walmart on the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Eagle Mountain City Council, 16 January 2024

12 motions · 13 ordinance lines · 10 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Rod Hess appointed to Planning Commission

The council seated Rod Hess on the Planning Commission for a three-year term through December 2026.

What it means: That seat reviews zoning changes and subdivision plans before they reach the council, so anyone building or living beside new development eventually meets its recommendations.

passed with a unanimous vote.

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to appoint Rod Hess to the Planning Commission for a three-year term through December2O26. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Senior advisory seat filled through 2027

The council appointed Karan Heffelfinger to the Senior Citizens Advisory Council for a four-year term ending in December 2027.

What it means: Seniors who use city programs gain one more voice advising the council. For everyone else, nothing shifts.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to appoint Karan Heffelfinger to the Senior Citizens Advisory Council for a four-year term through December 2027. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Adelle Curtis joins the Library Board

The council filled a Library Board seat with Adelle Curtis, a two-year term running through December 2025.

What it means: A volunteer seat gets an occupant. Library hours, cards and programs carry on unchanged.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to appoint Adelle Curtis to the Library Board for a two- year term through December 2O25. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Consent items approved, two pulled out

The council approved a consent agenda of bond releases, minutes and site plans, pulling one item out for separate discussion and sending a school site plan back over an easement.

What it means: Bundled housekeeping: warranty releases on finished subdivisions, past meeting minutes, and site plans staff already reviewed. The Alpine School District middle school plan returns later so the council can discuss vacating an easement.

passed with a unanimous vote.

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve the consent agenda removing Item 15.A. and placing it as a scheduled item and Instructing staff to bring item 14.8. back to the CIA Council to discuss vacation of the easement Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

City brand and marketing playbook adopted

The council adopted the city's brand and market playbook and asked for it to return at a later meeting once the additions raised in discussion are written in.

What it means: Signs, letterhead and city messaging follow from this document. No resident is asked to do anything.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve a resolution of Eagle Mountain, Utah, approuing the Eagle Mountain City Brand and Market Plagbook and requested that the Item be returned at a future meeting once the discussed additions have been Incorporated. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

City Center lot rezoned to neighborhood commercial

The council rezoned Plat 1 of City Center Commercial Phase A from Commercial C-1 to Commercial Neighborhood, picked over a broader zone after concerns about allowed uses.

What it means: What can open on that ground is now governed by the Commercial Neighborhood list, which councilmembers preferred to the wider Commercial Community zone. Households next to the site have the stake here.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

City Center commercial lot split approved

The council signed off on amending the recorded plat for Lot 1 of City Center Commercial Phase A, letting the owners carve out another commercial lot.

What it means: Owners of the site can now divide it and build on or sell the new piece. Homes backing onto the property get the partial screening wall finished once the site is developed.

passed with a unanimous vote.

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve the recorded plat amendment for City Center Commercial Phase A, Plat l, Lot l. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Casitas and garage apartments allowed

The city approved rules allowing a second dwelling inside a house, over a detached garage, or in a standalone casita, with parking for two vehicles required.

What it means: If you want a rental casita or a unit above your garage, this sets where it may go and requires two off-street spaces. Your lot's zoning still has to permit it.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it

Councilmember Wright seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

What it means: Wording cleanup inside the development code. Builders filing preliminary plats deal with it; nobody else will notice.

Hotel height and parking rules put on hold

The council delayed changes that would let hotels rise above the city's 50-foot limit and provide fewer parking stalls, waiting until the hotel feasibility study is finished.

What it means: No hotel was approved or blocked here. The current rules stand, so a developer wanting a four-story hotel still runs into the height cap and the stall-per-bed count until the study comes back.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to table an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section lZ35.O3O Land Use Table and 17.55.120 Tables until the Hotel Feasibility Study Is completed. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion. The motion passed with a vote of 4:1.

Less water required from builders per lot

The council cut the water a builder must hand the city per lot, dropping the indoor share from 0.53 to 0.34 acre-feet, and ordered a study of apartment water use.

What it means: Builders dedicating water rights for new subdivisions now hand over less, matching the smaller lawns the city already requires. Your own bill and service stay the same. The apartment study may bring the question back.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 16.35.130(a) Residential Water Rights Requirements and Instructed the CiA Engineer to perform a multifamily water usage study. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion. AMENDED MOTION: Councilmember Wood moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 1635.30(a) Residential Water Rfghts Requirements allowing staff to clarify on the table Planned Unit Development (PUD) water usage in non-multifamily communities and Instructed the City Engineer to perform a multifamily water usage study. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion. The motion passed with a vote of 4:1.

Council rules rewrite pushed to February

The council put off an ordinance spelling out its six-member form of government and how a mayor pro tempore is chosen, sending it back for rewording.

What it means: Nothing changes for you yet. The rules govern how councilmembers, staff and the mayor may speak for the city, and the reworked version was set to return on February 6.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to table an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Enacting Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 2.08, Form of Government and Section 2.15.120 Mayor Pro Tempore and Council Assignments to amend and clarify wording with the City Attorney and for the item to be on the agenda of the February 6,2024 City Council meeting. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

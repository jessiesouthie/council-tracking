Eagle Mountain City Council, 5 May 2026

10 motions · 12 ordinance lines · 17 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved

The council approved its consent agenda in a single vote, with a change to one item that had been discussed earlier in the work session.

What it means: This is routine housekeeping — the consent agenda is where the council batches items it does not need to debate one by one.

passed with a unanimous vote.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to approve the Consent Agenda with changes to Item 16.A. as discussed in Work Session . Councilmember Huish seconded the motion.

Draft 2026-27 budget accepted, hearings set

The council accepted the tentative city budget for 2026–2027 and set public hearings for June 16, 2026 and August 18, 2026.

What it means: No money is locked in yet. If you want a say in how the city spends next year, those two hearing dates are when to show up.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Craig WhitingYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Accepting the Eagle Mountain Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2026 —2027 and Setting a Public Hearing for the Interim Budget on for June 16, 2026 and Setting a Public Hearing for Budget Adoption on August 18, 2026. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Tax increase figures formally acknowledged

The council acknowledged the budget officer's statement of the proposed property tax increase and the schedule showing what that increase would cost taxpayers.

What it means: Nothing changes yet. This is a step state law requires before a city can raise property taxes; the real decision comes later at the public hearing.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Craig WhitingYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
  • Zachory HuishYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Acknowledging the Budget Officers Statement of a Proposed Property Tax Increase and Property Tax Impact Schedule (Truth-in-Taxation Compliance). Councilmember Whiting seconded the motion.

Property tax increase heads to a hearing

The council took up a resolution declaring the city's intent to raise property tax revenue, give public notice, and set the required Truth-in-Taxation hearing date.

What it means: If the increase goes ahead, homeowners would pay more. The Truth-in-Taxation hearing is the point where you can speak before the council settles on a final rate.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Craig WhitingYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
  • Zachory HuishYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Declaring Intent to Increase Property Tax Revenue, Providing Notice, and Setting a Public Hearing Date in Accordance with the Truth -in-Taxation Requirements. Councilmember Whiting seconded the motion.

Parks plan adopted, Sweetwater Park added

The city adopted its parks, trails and open space master plan along with the impact fee plan that pays for it, adding Sweetwater Park's cost to the list.

What it means: This is the city's list of parks and trails it intends to build, and the fees charged on new construction that fund them. Sweetwater Park is now part of that funded list.

passed with a unanimous vote.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Parks, Trails, and Open Space Master Plan and Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) with an update to include Sweetwater Park cost in the IFFP. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Design rules updated for shops and apartments

The council updated the city's design standards for commercial buildings and apartments, changing how new projects of that kind must be built and look.

What it means: Nothing changes for existing homes. The new standards shape the appearance of stores and apartment buildings approved from here on, and developers will have to design to them.

New parking and screening rules citywide

The city rewrote its parking and landscaping rules, requiring screening plants at least 3.5 feet tall when planted and vegetation that screens year-round.

What it means: This mostly affects builders and businesses planning new lots. Where a project sits next to homes, screening plants must already be 3.5 feet tall and stay green through the winter.

passed with a unanimous vote.

All sides where screening is required for a residential element be 3 ½ feet at time of planting and work with staff on proper placement of the monument sign so it is not shielded; and 3. Development deal set for Spring Run Plaza

The council approved the master development agreement for Spring Run Plaza, capping the required landscaping width at 10 feet and allowing engineered retaining walls taller than 6 feet without tiering.

What it means: This sets the rules the developer must build to at that corner. You may see tall, untiered retaining walls there, though stacked boulder and rock walls are not allowed.

passed with a 4:1 vote.

Councilmember Huish seconded the motion.

Spring Run Plaza approved on 27.35 acres

The city approved the layout for Spring Run Plaza, a 27.35-acre development at Spring Run Parkway and Cory B. Wride Memorial Highway, starting with six lots.

What it means: If you live or drive near that corner, expect construction on the first 8.15 acres. Two of the lots stay empty until the developer comes back to the city for approval.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Phase 1 (8.15 area) includes six lots, with Lots 7 and 8 remaining undeveloped with the understanding that when they are ready for development, the master development documents would be amended .

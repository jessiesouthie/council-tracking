SITE PLAN CONCEPT – Smith Ranch Community Park Councilmember Curtis said that a resident expressed concerns that the noise from the skate park will disturb wildlife in the area and suggested the decibel level of noise generated by skate parks be researched. Councilmember Curtis noted that the skate park would be over 500 feet from the kestrel box and the edge of the wash, and the distance from the road to the kestrel box is approximately 400 feet. He asked if the noise from the skate park would be louder than the noise generated by traffic. Councilmember Burnham asked how close the park would be to homes and expressed concern regarding noise nuisance. Councilmember Clark suggested planting trees and berms to mitigate the noise impact on wildlife. The space and other amenities between the skate park and homes would serve as a buffer and reduce the noise impact. Mr. Hickman said that the City conducted decimal tests for the skatepark at Pony Express Park and installed berms to reduce the noise impact. Aboveground metal ramps create more noise than enclosed concrete skate parks. He did not have data regarding the decibel level differences but can research the noise levels of each type of skate park. He verified that the proposed cost accounts for underground, concrete skate bowls and is estimated at $75 per square foot. Economic Development Director Aaron Sanborn said a study conducted by SPA Skateparks designer and contractor found that the noise levels of concrete skate parks range from 64 to 78 decibels, wooden skate parks range from 75 to 85 decibels, and steel-frame or modular skate parks range from 75 to 95 decibels. Councilmember Curtis stated for comparison that heavy traffic registers at 80 to 89 decibels and a vacuum cleaner or average radio produces approximately 75 decibels. Councilmember Burnham expressed concern that, although berms can help with noise mitigation, they can also obscure visibility and increase the chances of vandalism and other issues. Mr. Hickman said that outside of late-running sporting events, City parks close at 10:00 p.m. During the summer, the days are long enough for park amenities to not require lighting. Lighting would only be utilized during the spring and fall and the cost of the lighting compared to the usage time and benefit should be considered. Discussion ensued regarding skate park lighting safety benefits, usage impact, costs, potential concerns, the proximity and impact of the skate park to the closest homes, nighttime skating mitigation options and obstacles, and funding options.

Motion as recorded

passed with a unanimous vote.

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

Mayor Westmoreland recessed the meeting from 9:02 to 9:06 p.m. Councilmember Gray left the meeting due to technical difficulties at approximately 9:05 p.m. 14. STONEBRIDGE MONTESSORI 14.A. PUBLIC HEARING/ORDINANCE – An Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving a Rezone for Stonebridge Montessori. Mr. Mumford presented the item. The Ranches Master Development Plan has expired, thus a rezone is required. The Future Land Use Plan designation for the location is Community Commercial. The CC and CN zones are appropriate within the General Plan designation. Staff had recommended the building be turned 90 degrees to front along Ranches Parkway with the parking located behind the building. The Planning Commission supported the layout submitted by the applicant as it increases student safety. A three-and-a-half-foot headlight screen is required east and south of the parking lot. Originally staff requested a lane on the west side of the property extend north and connect to an access road on the neighboring property, as a master site plan and preliminary plat were approved for the northern property in 2008 for Saddle Junction, but has not been constructed. The applicant expressed safety concerns for the students should the northern connection be required. The road would benefit future projects but not the school. Should the road not be required for this project, it would need to be constructed entirely on the project to the west. The front entry faces towards the parking lot, not a main pedestrian way, as called for by EMMC 17.72.040(B). It is at the discretion of the City Council to determine if the articulation provided complies with standards found in EMMC 17.72.040(D). The Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the rezone with the condition that the property be rezoned to the Commercial Neighborhood (CN) rather than the Commercial Community (CC) zone. The Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the site plan with the following conditions: 1. The site plan shall be approved without a drive-aisle connecting the northern property; 2. The applicant shall provide proof that the stacking distances are adequate and will not cause traffic to back onto public right-of-way; 3. A 3½-foot high landscape berm shall be added around the perimeter of the parking area; and 4. The applicant shall provide additional directional signs in the parking lot. Applicant representative Tyler Dow confirmed that the coffee room indicated on the site plan was a breakroom for staff rather than a retail store. He explained he was building the school for a lessee that has run other Montessori schools. The multisensory educational practices at this location would follow the same model. Traffic attendants will be on site to help with pedestrian safety, traffic flow, and stacking. A right-in turn is anticipated to provide onsite stacking rather than the two-directional traffic flow indicated on the site plan. The staggered start times for the classes should reduce the traffic congestion. The area in the southeast corner of the plan is a stormwater detention area. City Engineer Chris Trusty said the school access is spaced away from Ranches Parkway and the applicant has attempted to accommodate sufficient stacking distances to avoid traffic backing onto Ranches Parkway. Councilmember Love stated that as the adjacent locations will be commercial uses, a north-south drive aisle will be necessary and expressed concern that allowing the applicant to exclude the drive aisle for this project will cause design and access alignment difficulties during the future development of the adjacent parcels. Mr. Dow said they desire to provide as large of an outdoor play area as possible to support the multisensory learning experiences of the students. The Planning Commission felt that preventing traffic from passing along the west side of the school and playground would increase the safety of the children. He requested the burden of the north-south connection be placed on the developer of the property to the west. Councilmember Curtis expressed concern that the plan did not show any landscaping on the east side of the school. Discussion ensued regarding a northern access road, ownership of the various properties, master development of the area, and Municipal Code connectivity requirements. Mayor Westmoreland opened the public hearing at 9:40 p.m. As there were no comments, Mayor Westmoreland closed the hearing. Mr. Mumford stated Municipal Code requires all parking and other vehicular use areas be interconnected with adjacent properties, but access to all four sides of a property is not required. Discussion ensued regarding public schools not being required to connect to commercial properties, emergency access considerations, the type of connection to provide, the proposed and potential alignment of the connection to the north, the vested rights of the Saddle Junction plan’s road connections, possibly requiring the applicant to provide a half-width of the drive aisle, and considerations of the benefit and burden of being required to provide a partial or full drive aisle. Councilmember Gray returned to the meeting at 9:50 p.m. Discussion ensued regarding the problems and benefits of the curvature of the proposed alignment, the area indicated for the road versus paved parking, directing staff to review the plan to determine an appropriate alignment and access with the applicant, and the impact to the proposed design to accommodating the drive aisle. Mr. Dow requested that should the Council determine to burdened them with the entire length of the drive aisle, for the minimum width possible to be located on their property. Discussion ensued clarifying direction to staff to work with the applicant to provide a sensible alignment between the property and the road south of the Maverik to the north of the property.

Motion as recorded

passed with a unanimous vote.

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it

Eagle Mountain City Council, 17 September 2024

13 motions · 27 ordinance lines · 13 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved, three items pulled

The council approved its consent agenda, dropping one item and pulling three others — including the wayfinding signs and the affordable housing plan — out for full discussion.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is the procedural step that groups routine business into a single vote and sets aside a few items for debate.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the Consent Agenda, removing Item 12.E. from the agenda, and moving Items 11.B., 12.A, and 12.C. to the scheduled items for further discussion. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Eagle Square commercial development approved

The council approved the preliminary layout for the Eagle Square commercial development, requiring the traffic study be used to place its entrance off Pony Express Parkway.

What it means: A commercial development moves ahead on Pony Express Parkway. Its south entrance must be sited using the traffic study so it lines up with the intersection to the south.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the Eagle Square Commercial Preliminary Plat with the following condition: 1. The traffic study be utilized to locate the entrance off of Pony Express Parkway into the south portion of the commercial development, recognizing it is located on the southeast portion of the preliminary plat and aligns with intersection to the south. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Six wayfinding signs from Impact Signs

The council took up an agreement with Impact Signs to supply six wayfinding signs; the motion doesn't state a cost.

What it means: If it moves ahead, you'd see six new directional signs around the city. Nothing else changes.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving an Agreement for Six Wayfinding Signs with Impact Signs. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Affordable housing plan put off

The council delayed adopting the city's affordable housing plan, sending it back for the changes members discussed before it returns at a future meeting.

What it means: Nothing changes for now. The plan is the city's road map for housing people can afford, and it comes back to the council at a later meeting.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayExcused
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to table a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting the Eagle Mountain City Affordable Housing Plan, to be brought to a future meeting with the changes the Council discussed. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

City map redrawn for future shops and parks

The council redrew its long-range land use map for certain Ranches and Eagle Mountain Properties parcels, marking most for community commercial and several for parks and open space.

What it means: This is the planning step that lines the city's official map up with the zoning change on those same parcels. For neighbors, it points to shops, parks and open space instead of more housing.

What it means: If you live near these parcels in The Ranches, expect shops and commercial buildings on most of them rather than houses, with a few kept as farmland or open space.

Rose Ranch farmland-to-housing rezone

The council weighed rezoning Rose Ranch from agriculture to the R2 and R3 housing zones, conditioned on crosswalks, a historical marker and finishing the Pony Express Trail.

What it means: If it goes ahead, farmland at Rose Ranch becomes housing. Neighbors would get raised tabletop crosswalks and a historical marker on the Pony Express Trail, and the trail finished as usable space.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Master Development Plan and Rezoning Certain Lands Known as Rose Ranch from Agriculture to the R2 and R3 Zones with the following conditions: 1. Add tabletop crosswalks on the Pony Express Trail and a historical marker; and 2. Complete the Pony Express Trail as a finished space. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Street width and sidewalk rules put on hold

The council delayed a decision on updated street cross-section rules, asking staff to return with recommendations on curb, gutter and a sidewalk for rural 50-foot streets.

What it means: No change yet. Whatever the council settles on would shape how new streets are built — mainly affecting new subdivisions, not existing ones.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

What it means: Nothing changes for homeowners or builders — the existing residential zoning rules remain in force. The motion doesn't say what the rejected changes would have done.

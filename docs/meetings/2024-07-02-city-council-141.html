All meetings / 2 July 2024

Eagle Mountain City Council, 2 July 2024

7 motions · 13 ordinance lines · 15 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Three items pulled for fuller discussion

The council pulled the Eagle Square plat, the wayfinding sign contract, and the marketing contract out of the consent agenda for separate discussion.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is the procedural step that moved three items out of the quick-approval batch so they could be debated individually.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve the Consent Agenda, moving items 12.A. Eagle Square Preliminary Plat, 13.A. A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving an Agreement with Identity Signs for Six Wayfinding Signs, and 13.D. A Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving a Professional Services Agreement with REDKOR Brand Campaigns to Create a Marketing Plan to the Scheduled Items for further discussion. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Eagle Square shopping center plat delayed

The council delayed a decision on the Eagle Square commercial preliminary plat until legal wording in the related development agreement is sorted out.

What it means: Work on this commercial project cannot move forward until the council takes it up again. Nothing changes for residents in the meantime.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to table the Eagle Square Commercial Preliminary Plat, with instruction to staff to work out the legal issues with the language in the Third Amendment to the Master Development Agreement, or to bring the Amendment to the Council in conjunction with the Preliminary Plat. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

New park and city signs sent back to staff

The council rejected a contract with Identity Signs for six new wayfinding signs and told staff to find sturdier materials and cheaper installation.

What it means: The aging signs directing you to parks like Smith Ranch Park stay as they are for now, and new ones will not go up this fall as staff had hoped.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to deny the approval of a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, for an Agreement with Identity Signs for Six Wayfinding Signs with instructions to staff to find a more durable material for the signs and a lower installation cost option. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

City hires marketing firm at $3,500 a month

The city hired REDKOR Brand Campaigns to build a city marketing plan for $3,500 a month, cancellable at any time and capped at 14 months.

What it means: Nothing changes day to day. Over time you may notice more consistent city messaging about tourism and business, and the city can end the contract whenever it wants.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve of a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving a Professional Services Agreement with REDKOR Brand Campaigns to Create a Marketing Plan, with a month-to-month plan of $3,500 per month that can be discontinued at any time, with a benchmark plan with economic portion of the scope of work frontloaded, and not to exceed 14 months. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Mayor and council pay raised, starting January

The city set the mayor's pay at $119,800 plus a $600 monthly car allowance and council pay at $18,100 a year, effective January 1, 2025.

What it means: This is taxpayer money going to the city's elected officials. The new amounts take effect January 1, 2025; there is no direct change to your bills or services.

passed with a vote of 4:1.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkNo
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapters 2.10 Mayor and 2.15 City Council Regarding Elected Official Compensation, with the following changes: 1. EMMC 2.10.020(B)(1) is amended to, “Mayor’s annual compensation as a primary source of income: $127,000.” 2. EMMC 2.10.020(B)(2) is amended to, “Mayor’s annual compensation as a secondary source of income: $30,470 plus $600 per month transportation allowance.” 3. EMMC 2.15.010(A) is amended to, “Each member of the city council shall be paid annual compensation in the amount of $20,500 and shall be entitled to receive other benefits that are provided by the city or incidental to the city council member’s duties such as a computer (tablet), conference fees, professional memberships, etc.” 4. EMMC 2.15.010(B) is removed in its entirety. 5. The effective date be January 1, 2025. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion. AMENDED MOTION: Councilmember Wright moved to approve Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapters 2.10 Mayor and 2.15 City Council Regarding Elected Official Compensation, with the following changes: 1. EMMC 2.10.020(B)(1) is amended to, “Mayor’s annual compensation as a primary source of income: $119,800 plus $600.00 per month transportation allowance.” 2. EMMC 2.10.020(B)(2) is amended to, “Mayor’s annual compensation as a secondary source of income: $30,470 plus $600 per month transportation allowance.” 3. EMMC 2.15.010(A) is amended to, “Each member of the city council shall be paid annual compensation in the amount of $18,100 and shall be entitled to receive other benefits that are provided by the city or incidental to the city council member’s duties such as a computer (tablet), conference fees, professional memberships, etc.” 4. The effective date be January 1, 2025. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Fence and retaining wall rules put on hold

The council delayed a decision on new rules for privacy fences, landscaping buffers, and retaining walls.

What it means: Nothing changes for now — the current fence and wall rules still apply. If you were waiting on the new standards before building, you will have to wait longer.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 16.35.090 Privacy Fencing, Chapter 17.10 Definitions, and Chapter 17.60 Landscaping Buffering Fencing and Transitioning, and Enacting Chapter 17.64 Retaining Walls. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Historic zones rules delayed again

The council put off a decision on new Historic Zone rules for a second time, pushing it to the August 6, 2024 meeting.

What it means: Nothing changes yet. If you own one of the older properties the city wants to bring under 2024 development rules, the decision is still pending.

passed with a vote of 4:1.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamNo
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Enacting Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.32, Historic Zones, to be brought back to the August 6, 2024 City Council meeting. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

All meetings / 6 February 2024

Eagle Mountain City Council, 6 February 2024

11 motions · 19 ordinance lines · 1 resolution line

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved

Everything grouped on the consent agenda passed in a single vote, with the record here not listing the individual items it held.

What it means: Consent items are the ones no councilmember pulled out for debate. Nothing on this list reaches you.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the consent agenda. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Parkway Fields phases and layout redrawn

The council approved a redrawn Parkway Fields plan that splits the townhome phase in two and adds about four townhomes, offset by fewer lots in a later phase.

What it means: The number of homes on the site does not go up. If you are buying there, phase and plat labels changed, and the developer told the council it will dedicate land along Old Airport Road for a trail and trailhead.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wr@ht moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending the Parkway Fields Master Development Plan as presented. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Parkway Fields development agreement amended

The council updated the master development agreement behind Parkway Fields, the Ault Farms project, so the contract matches the site's revised plan.

What it means: This is the contract that fixes what the builder owes the city there. Buyers and neighbors in Parkway Fields are the ones it reaches; the rest of the city will not notice.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending the Parkway Fields Master Development Agreement Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Residential development standards amended

The council amended its residential development standards, section 17.25.040, though the minutes do not record what the amendment changed.

What it means: Rules for what goes into new subdivisions moved here, but the record is too thin to say how it lands on any particular street.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 17.25.040 Residential Development Standards. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Building and construction standards updated

The council adopted an updated Title 15, the city's building and construction standards, which now carries truck route approval requirements.

What it means: Contractors and engineers filing plans build to the new standards from here. For everyone else this is a technical manual you will never open.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to approve an ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Title 15 Buildings and Construction Standards and Specifications. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion. The motion passed with a unanimous vote.

Fence and buffer rules rewritten

The city rewrote its fencing rules, declaring broken, flammable or spite fences unlawful nuisances, adding a split-rail definition, and barring berms or hedges where a wall is required.

What it means: A fence that is falling apart, or one put up to needle a neighbor, can now be treated as a nuisance and ordered fixed. Staff will bring back separate changes on commercial buffers and fences where the ground drops away.

passed with a vote of 4:1.

What it means: Businesses that sell alcohol are the ones who deal with this section. The record is too thin to say what is different for them.

Lot size rules sent back to staff

The council delayed changes to lot size mixture and residential development standards, sending staff to collect councilmember feedback before a March 6 return.

What it means: Builders waiting on the new lot rules wait another month. Anyone with an opinion on lot sizes gets another chance to speak when the item comes back March 6.

Council limits mayor's power to set policy

The council wrote into code that neither the mayor nor staff may enact city policy without a majority council vote, and that no member may speak for the full body.

What it means: City policy now has to clear a public council vote before it takes effect, so more of it happens in front of you at a meeting. Liaison assignments also go to a council vote rather than the mayor alone.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The council handed each member board assignments for the year, from the cemetery and arts boards to the Planning Commission and the Utah League of Cities and Towns.

What it means: If you sit on one of those boards, you now know which councilmember will be in the room with you. For everyone else this is an internal calendar.

Jared Gray named mayor pro tempore

The council picked Councilmember Jared Gray as mayor pro tempore, the member who steps in when the mayor cannot preside.

What it means: Nothing changes for households. The title matters only on the rare night the mayor is away from the dais.

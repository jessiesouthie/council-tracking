Hansen retaining wall variance

The commission took up a motion to let the Hansen property keep a fence and retaining wall that breaks city rules, citing problems created by a neighbor.

What it means: The record does not show how the vote came out. Either way, it concerns one homeowner's wall, and the motion itself says the general public is not affected.

(outcome not found)

  • Jason AllenYes
  • Robert FoxYes
  • Rod HessExcused
The motion as the minutes record it
Commissioner Whiting moved to grant Item 7.D., Hansen Retaining Wall Variance Request with the given findings: • Relative to Criteria 1 - Literal enforcement of this title would cause an unreasonable hardship for the applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of this title: the applicant’s neighbor caused undue hardship on the applicant that caused the applicant to come up with a current solution with the fence/retaining wall. • Relative to Criteria 2 - There are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply to other properties in the same district: the applicant’s neighbor caused undue hardship on the applicant that caused the applicant to come up with a current solution with the fence/retaining wall. • Relative to Criteria 3 - Granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of a substantial property right possessed by other property in the same district: that the enjoyment of his property was affected by what the neighbor had done, solving it through what the applicant did with this wall. • Relative to Criteria 4 - The variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to the public interest: there is not much that needs to be justified there, as it does not affect the general public, and not contrary to the public interest. • Relative to Criteria 5 - The spirit of this title is observed and substantial justice is done: This title is observed, substantial justice is done. Commissioner Fox seconded the motion.

What it means: Nothing is final until the council votes, including how far the building will sit from the property line. Neighbors of the site have the most reason to follow the council vote.

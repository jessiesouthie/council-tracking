All meetings / 16 September 2025

Eagle Mountain City Council, 16 September 2025

7 motions · 7 ordinance lines · 10 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved, one item pulled

Everything on the consent agenda passed in a single vote except item 14.A, which the council moved to scheduled items for its own discussion.

What it means: Batching uncontroversial items into one vote is how the council saves floor time. The step touches nobody outside the meeting room.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to approve the Consent Agenda removing item 14.A. to be placed on the Scheduled Items for further discussion. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Development deal with Mountainland Supply approved

The city approved a development agreement with Mountainland Supply; the motion itself does not state the site, the size, or the terms.

What it means: A development agreement locks in what a builder may do on a specific piece of land. The motion doesn't say where that land is or what is going up, so the details sit in the agreement itself.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving a Development Agreement with Mountainland Supply. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Half-price offer of $45,000 an acre rejected

The council rejected a lower price for the same parcel, $45,000 per acre or $1.03 per square foot, noting it needs no additional water rights.

What it means: Had this one carried, the city would have taken less than half as much per acre for the land. It did not, so the higher figure stands.

failed for lack of a majority vote, 1:4.

  • Brett WrightNo
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayNo
  • Melissa ClarkNo
  • Rich WoodNo
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Authorizing the Disposal of Significant City-Owned Real Property Pursuant to Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 3.30 and using the price of the land at $45,000 per acre ($1.03 per square foot) nothing the parcel does not require additional water rights. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Bid to pause the land sale turned down

Council members declined to hold off on the land sale while staff tightened water requirements and wrote a policy for reviewing future purchase requests.

What it means: A postponement that does not happen leaves nothing for you to notice. The sale itself was decided in a separate vote.

failed for lack of a majority vote, 2:3.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamNo
  • Jared GrayNo
  • Melissa ClarkNo
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to table a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Authorizing the Disposal of Significant City-Owned Real Property Pursuant to Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 3.30 and directed Staff to work on refining water requirements and draft a policy to ensure consistent review of future Land Disposal Applications. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

City land sold at $95,000 an acre

The council cleared the sale of a significant piece of city-owned real property at $95,000 per acre, or $2.18 per square foot.

What it means: This settles what the buyer pays, not what goes on the ground. The motion says nothing about future use, so what neighbors will eventually see remains open.

passed with a 3:2 vote.

  • Brett WrightNo
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodNo
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Authorizing the Disposal of Significant City-Owned Real Property Pursuant to Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 3.30 with a sales price of $95,000 per acre ($2.18 per square foot). Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

City to draft telecom rules

The council added a telecommunications ordinance, a franchise agreement, or both to its list of legislative priorities for staff to work on.

What it means: No rule exists yet, so nothing changes for phone or internet customers. Whatever staff draft would have to come back to the council before it means anything.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to add drafting a Telecommunications Ordinance and/or Franchise Agreement to the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Rules wanted for selling off city land

The council put a written policy for judging applications to buy city-owned land on its list of legislative priorities, so staff review such requests consistently.

What it means: Nothing changes for you today. If the policy gets written, anyone asking to buy a piece of city ground would face one standard instead of a case-by-case judgment call.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to add drafting a policy for code adoption to guide Staff in evaluating Land Disposal Applications to the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

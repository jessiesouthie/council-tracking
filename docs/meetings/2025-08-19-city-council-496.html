Motions and roll-call votes

Susan Allen joins the Senior Advisory Board

The council appointed Susan Allen to the Senior Advisory Board for a term running through December 31, 2025.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this fills a seat on a volunteer advisory board through the end of the year.

Consent agenda approved, two items pulled

The council approved its consent agenda but pulled the Transportation Master Plan and a fee reduction request out for separate discussion.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — this is the housekeeping step that clears routine items in one vote and sets two aside for fuller debate.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Transportation Master Plan adopted

The council adopted the Transportation Master Plan, the city's long-range blueprint for which roads Eagle Mountain builds and widens as it grows.

What it means: Nothing changes on your street tomorrow, but this plan is what the city points to when deciding which roads get built or widened and what developers must put in.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting the Transportation Master Plan. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Discount on a rezone fee turned down

The council declined Steve McCleery's request to cut the fee for his rezone application from $1,350 to $760.

What it means: Only the applicant is affected — he pays the full $1,350. The city's rezone application fee is unchanged for everyone else.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to deny a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Fee Reduction Request of Steve McCleery for a Rezone Application from $1,350 to $760. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Subdivision plat rules amended

The council amended the city's rules for preliminary and final plats — the approval steps land must clear before it can be split into lots.

What it means: This changes the process for developers and anyone subdividing land; the motion does not spell out what the new rules require. Existing homeowners see no change.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 16.20 Preliminary Plats and 16.25 Final Plats. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Site plan rules updated for new development

The council updated the city's site plan rules but left out a proposed change covering the swap of one approved exterior building material for another.

What it means: This touches builders and businesses going through site plan review, who must still get sign-off to change approved exterior materials. If you are not developing property, nothing changes for you.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.100 Site Plans and omitting the proposed redline in 17.100.130 section “D” number nine (changing the exterior finishing materials from one acceptable material to another acceptable material as listen in the standards in the zone). Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

Youth Council rules adopted with mayor voting

With the mayor included in the vote, the council adopted the rewritten Youth Council rules, shifting appointment of youth members to a committee of advisors.

What it means: This affects the teens on the Youth Council and how they are chosen. Nothing changes for other residents.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Repealing and Reenacting Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 2.45 Youth Council with changes as follows: • Include “Youth” Mayor when referring to the Youth Mayor and “Mayor” when referring to the appointed City Mayor, • In Section 2.45.040 Subsection E, committee approval by the City Manager will be replaced by Youth Council Committee of Advisors. • In Section 2.45.040 Subsection A.8., include “Elected City Mayor” when coordinating all activities with the Youth Council Committee of Advisors. • In 2.45.010 Section C: Change from “members are appointed to Youth Council by the City Manager” to “members are appointed by the Youth Council Committee of Advisors”. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

City to sell a small parcel to adjoining owners

The city approved selling 0.156 acres of city-owned land to the owners of the neighboring lots, with the sale to be finished by February 19, 2026.

What it means: Only the owners of the adjoining lots are affected. They can buy the parcels together on one application, and within six months their fences must sit on the correct lot line or be removed.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Approving the Disposal of 0.156 Acres of Designated "Significant Real Property" (Utah County Parcel #49:601:0080) Pursuant to EMMC 3.30.060 and 3.30.070, and Establishing Terms of the Disposal with terms below: • Approving option two with each landowner of the parcels listed signing the land disposal application. The applicants may combine the application into one land disposal application and fee. • If any landowner, in any of the lots are unwilling or unable to purchase the corresponding parcels, the approval will revert to option one. • It is required that all fences are on the correct lot line or the lots are to have no fence within 6 months of recordation. • The water requirements for each parcel are fulfilled with the purchase of the parcels as deemed appropriate by staff. • The full land disposal application and process must be finalized in six months (February 19, 2026). Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

RAP tax question goes to voters in November

The council put a 0.1% sales tax for recreation, arts, and cultural facilities on the November 4, 2025 ballot.

What it means: You decide this one at the polls. If it passes, you would pay an extra 10 cents on every $100 spent in the city, with the money going to recreation and cultural facilities.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Providing for a Referendum to be held on November 4, 2025, for the purpose of submitting to the qualified voters of Eagle Mountain City, Utah County, Utah the question of authorization of the imposition a 0.1% sales and use tax (one-tenth of one percent) for the purpose of funding recreational, cultural, botanical, and zoological facilities, and botanical, cultural, and zoological organizations within the City (RAP Tax). Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

What it means: Nothing changes yet — this only tells state lawmakers what Eagle Mountain wants. Any actual home-buying help for veterans and military families would have to come from the Legislature.

Eagle Mountain City Council, 6 November 2024

5 motions · 4 ordinance lines · 9 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved

The council approved its consent agenda, the batch of items voted through together without separate discussion.

What it means: This is routine housekeeping — nothing changes for residents.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Clark moved to approve the Consent Agenda. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

New easement rules added to the land use code

The city added a new easements chapter to its land use code and updated the code's definitions to match.

What it means: This mainly touches property owners and developers dealing with easements — the strips of land set aside for things like utilities or access. Most residents will see no change day to day.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 17.10.030 Definitions and Enacting Chapter 17.59 Easements. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

No decision on city facilities master plan

The council did not adopt a Facilities Master Plan; a proposal to treat the Galloway report as a guideline for a future plan went no further.

What it means: The city still has no adopted long-range plan for its buildings and facilities. Nothing changes for residents right now.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, accepting the Galloway report as a guideline for future adoption of the Eagle Mountain City Facilities Master Plan. The motion died for a lack of a second.

Public art plan adopted

The city adopted a Public Art Plan to guide how Eagle Mountain approaches art in public places.

What it means: Nothing changes day to day. The plan sets the city's general approach; any specific artwork or spending would still need its own approval.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting the Eagle Mountain City Public Art Plan. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Residential zoning rules queued for review

The council added a review of the city's residential zoning rules, Municipal Code Chapter 17.25, to its list of legislative priorities.

What it means: Nothing changes yet — this only puts residential zoning on the council's to-do list. Any actual change to what can be built in a residential zone would be a separate decision later.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes

  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Gray moved to add a review of Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Chapter 17.25 Residential Zones to the Legislative Priorities List. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

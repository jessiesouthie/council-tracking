Eagle Mountain City Council, 18 February 2025

13 motions · 16 ordinance lines · 16 resolution lines

Motions and roll-call votes

Emily Buss joins the Library Advisory Board

The council appointed Emily Buss to the Library Advisory Board to finish a three-year term running through December 2027.

What it means: The board advises on library matters. Filling the seat has no effect on hours, fees, or services you use.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to appoint Emily Buss to the Library Advisory Board, completing a three-year term through December 2027. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Four items pulled off the consent agenda

The council approved its consent agenda but pushed two sets of minutes to a later meeting and pulled the Firefly water tank change order and a Pole Canyon annexation out for discussion.

What it means: Two items residents may care about, a water tank cost change and the annexation of 178 acres into the Pole Canyon district, were moved out for open debate rather than passed in a batch.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to approve the Consent Agenda, moving items 13.A. January 7, 2025 Minutes, and 13.B. January 21, 2025 Minutes to a future Council meeting agenda, and moving 12.A. Firefly 4MG Tank Change Order #2, and 14.C. A Resolution Consenting to the Annexation of 178 Acres of Land into the Pole Canyon Basic Local District to the Scheduled Items for further discussion. Councilmember Gray seconded the motion.

$190K more for the Firefly water tank

The council took up a change order adding $189,985.67 to the cost of the Firefly 4-million-gallon water tank.

What it means: This adds money to a city water storage tank already under contract. The motion sets no change to your water bill, and the record does not show a final outcome.

178 acres proposed for Pole Canyon district

The council took up a resolution consenting to the annexation of 178 acres of land into the Pole Canyon Basic Local District.

What it means: This concerns the 178 acres in question and which district serves them, not existing neighborhoods. The meeting record does not show how this one ended.

City backs an inland port area in Eagle Mountain

The council voted to support creating a Utah Inland Port Authority project area inside Eagle Mountain, endorsing state involvement in local development.

What it means: An inland port area lets a state authority direct development and tax money within its boundaries. The motion does not name the location or acreage, so watch for the boundary to be set later.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wood moved to adopt a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Supporting the Creation of a Utah Inland Port Authority Project Area in Eagle Mountain City. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

New rules for fences and screening

The council rewrote the section of city code covering general fencing and screening, changing the rules that apply when property owners put up a fence or wall.

What it means: If you are planning a fence at your home or business, check the updated code before you build. The requirements are not the same as they were.

passed with a majority vote of 4:1.

  • Rich WoodNo
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code Section 17.60.120 General Fencing and Screening Provisions as presented. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion.

Impact fee cut for new businesses

The city partially suspended its transportation impact fee for nonresidential development, and dropped the carve-out that had applied to restaurants at every building size.

What it means: If you are building a business, store, or office in Eagle Mountain, you will pay less in road impact fees. The restaurant carve-out no longer applies at any size.

passed with a unanimous vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Donna BurnhamYes
  • Jared GrayYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Burnham moved to adopt an Ordinance Partially Suspending the Collection of the Transportation Impact Fee for Nonresidential Developments with the changes of removing the restaurant exception from all building sizes. Councilmember Wright seconded the motion.

The council approved the fifth amendment to the 2024-2025 city budget, shifting spending inside the current fiscal year.

What it means: The motion names no dollar figure or department, so there is no specific change a resident would feel from it. This is the city truing up its books mid-year.

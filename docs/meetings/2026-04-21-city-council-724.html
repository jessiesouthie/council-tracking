Motions and roll-call votes

Consent agenda approved, one item held back

The council approved its consent agenda of grouped items, pulling item 10.A. out and setting it aside for later.

What it means: Nothing changes for residents — the consent agenda is the batch of uncontested items the council passes in a single vote.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Councilmember Wright moved to approve the Consent Agenda , with Item 10.A. removed and tabled. Councilmember Wood seconded the motion.

Motion to delay the land decision withdrawn

A move to postpone the same land decision, with direction to work out a better solution with neighboring owners and city staff, was withdrawn.

What it means: Nothing was decided here — the motion was taken back before any vote, so it changes nothing for residents.

Outcome not recorded

No roll call was recorded for this motion.

Councilmember Wood moved to table a Resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Considering the Disposal of a Portion of Utah County Parcel #46:536:0430, designated as “Significant Real Property” Pursuant to EMMC 3.30.060 and 3.30.070 and directed the applicant to work with neighboring property owners, the City Attorney, and the Recorder’s Office to identify a better solution . City won't sell off a piece of its land

The council rejected a proposal to dispose of part of a city-owned parcel that the city code treats as significant real property.

Screening rules for apartment buildings delayed

The council delayed a decision on rules for screening heat pumps and other equipment on apartment buildings, sending it back for revisions.

What it means: Nothing changes right now. The rewritten rules will come back to the council later, and they mainly affect apartment builders rather than homeowners.

passed with a unanimous vote.

Councilmember Wood moved to table an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code section 17.72.040 Screening for Mu lti-Family Buildings , with direction to revise provisions related to heat pumps and screening standards , review spacing requirements to ensure proper airflow, incorporate the Planning Commission’s redline revisions, and return the item as a consent agenda item. Councilmember Clark seconded the motion.

Stricter rules for steel fences

The city set new standards for steel fencing, requiring weathering steel at least 1/8 inch thick, banning corrugated panels, and dropping the annual sealing rule.

What it means: If you put up a steel fence, the city will now check the metal's thickness when you pull your permit, corrugated panels are no longer allowed, and you no longer have to seal it every year.

passed with a 4:1 vote.

  • Brett WrightYes
  • Craig WhitingYes
  • Melissa ClarkYes
  • Rich WoodNo
  • Zachory HuishYes
The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Wright moved to adopt an Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Amending the Eagle Mountain Municipal Code section 17.60 and 17.10 Regarding Fencing with the following amendments being made: 1. Require Corten ( weathering ) steel to be a minimum of 1/8 inch thick and add this requirement to Section 17.60.120; and 2. Prohibit corrugated material of any type under Section 17.60.120(1)(C); and 3. Remove the annual sealing requirement ; and 4. Require a drip edge where steel panels are in close proximity to concrete; and 5. Require verification during the permitting process that the steel meets chapter requirements. Councilmember Whiting seconded the motion.

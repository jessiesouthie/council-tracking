The motion as the minutes record it
Councilmember Love moved to approve a resolution of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, amending the Policies and Procedures Manual regarding continuing education reimbursement and paydays. Councilmember Burnham seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Donna Burnham, Melissa Clark, Colby Curtis, and Carolyn Love. The motion passed with a unanimous vote. This portion of the discussion occurred after item 17. Ms. Kofoed informed the Council that the City received an anonymous comment from an employee regarding the proposal after the item had concluded and noted that the item did not include a public hearing. The employee explained that job certifications were previously covered 100% by the City and the approved amendment would now require employees to pay 25% of the cost. Councilmember Clark recommended excluding certifications from being required to be funded 25% by the employees. She had not realized the amendment would negatively impact certifications and does not want to penalize employees. Ms. Kofoed explained that the City has a portion of funding specified for employee travel and training and these funds would be in addition to that budget. Training programs are recommended by supervisors and approved by human resources if funding is available. She expressed her support of the City covering the full cost of certification programs, if funding is available. Mr. Jerome said the travel and training program supports employee certifications and continuing education credits through attending conferences and other trainings. He explained that Ms. Valenzuela’s analysis found the City was offering lower continuing education benefits than other cities and the proposal is a median benefit option compared to other programs. Some cities have a tuition reimbursement percentage cap. The Council could elect to remove the 75% coverage and offer to reimburse up to $2,500 per employee per year. He is a strong supporter of education and sees it as a mutual benefit for the City in promoting employee continuing education. Councilmembers Love and Curtis expressed apprehension to make changes to the proposal without discussing them with Ms. Valenzuela. Ms. Kofoed stated the proposal was discussed during staff meeting and she has correspondence from Ms. Valenzuela expressing her preference to remove the requirement for employees to pay 25%. During the pandemic, programs were offered that expedited the certification process, and the reimbursement program assisted employees in utilizing the opportunities made available.

ORDINANCE/PUBLIC HEARING – An Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting the Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget and Certified Tax Rate. Ms. Ruesch explained the importance of the budget as a mechanism to further the policies and visions established by the Council that make Eagle Mountain a city residents choose to make their homes. Changes to the proposed budget since the last meeting include over $30,000,000 of investment in capital infrastructure development including the second phase of Airport Road, which staff will work aggressively to complete. Maintaining the current level of service provided by the City requires funding for an additional approximately 12.5 FTEs. Councilmember Curtis stated he has been open with his objection to the Wasatch Wing and Clay road and requested verification that the project is excluded from this budget. Mr. Mumford clarified the Wasatch Wing and Clay road will require an interlocal agreement with the County, which will require separate Council approval. Ms. Ruesch stated the item includes the adoption of the certified tax rate for the City, which has declined from the previous year. The certified tax rate lowers as property values increase. The City experienced an increase of approximately $200,000 in property tax revenue due to new growth. Mr. Jerome verified the tax rate has lowered each year over the past four years and is currently the lowest rate in City history. ORDINANCE/PUBLIC HEARING – An Ordinance of Eagle Mountain City, Utah, Adopting an Impact Fee Enactment for a Public Safety Impact Fee; Providing for the Calculation and Collection of Such Fees; Providing for Appeal, Accounting, and Severability of the Same and Other Related Matters. Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham Vice President Fred Philpot presented the item and explained the impact fee process. The Impact Facility Plan and Impact Fee Analysis process considered the service area and demand, level of service (LOS), existing facilities and buy-in, future facilities, financing, and proportionate share analysis. The police proportionate share analysis determined a total impact fee cost of $11,109,356 and an $820,971 cost to impact fee. The proposed police impact fee schedule based upon a $66 cost per call and 0.633 residential calls per unit and 2.190 non-residential calls per unit, per 1,000 square feet, resulted in a proposed impact fee of $42 for residential and $145 for non-residential units. Impact fees for non-standard developments will be calculated separately based upon the $66 cost per call and the number of calls per calculation per unit for the non-standard use type. The next steps include holding the public hearing noticed for this meeting; the Council adopting, modifying, or rejecting the proposed impact fees; and a 90-day wait period prior to implementing an approved impact fee increase. Mr. Philpot explained that the proposed $42 residential impact is lower than the typical $150 up to $350 range found in other communities. The lower impact fee is due to the low square footage LOS. The City can reevaluate the LOS after constructing new facilities and adjust the impact fee. Mayor Westmoreland opened the public hearing at 8:50 p.m. As there were no comments, Mayor Westmoreland closed the hearing.

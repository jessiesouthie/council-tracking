## What was discussed

### 3.A — Resolution protesting the Red Cedar Corporation annexation petition

Clark opened by explaining why residents were being called in on a Wednesday afternoon: "It was something that we needed to call a special meeting for because of some timelines and deadlines." Red Cedar Corporation filed a petition with the Town of Cedar Fort on July 27 asking that a block of unincorporated Utah County land be annexed into that town. Marcus told the council the city only learned of the filing the day before. State law required the petitioner to send Eagle Mountain a notice of intent before filing at all, and no such notice arrived. That, he said, is one of the procedural problems with the petition.

Eagle Mountain's objection rests on two things. The land overlaps the area the city has already mapped as its own future growth area, and the city has "some interest in potentially annexing a portion of that proposed annexation." Beyond the city's own plans, Marcus said several of the landowners caught inside the proposed boundary have been in touch and "do not desire to be annexed into Cedar Fort and are very troubled by this petition." Because the city is an affected entity, it has standing to protest, and the resolution before the council was the vehicle for doing so.

Huish asked what happens next. The protest goes to the Utah County Clerk, Marcus said, after which the Boundary Commission must set a public hearing where Eagle Mountain can make its case. He was candid that the commission's recommendation "is not actually binding on the town of Cedar Fort," though it would "bear a lot of persuasive weight," and that the city would have to weigh other legal options if Cedar Fort pressed ahead anyway. Steve pushed on the calendar, reading state code to mean Cedar Fort could still hold a September 15 hearing. Marcus first said the hearing could not go forward, then accepted Steve's sharper reading: Cedar Fort may hold the hearing and deny the petition, which would end the matter, but it cannot approve it while the protest is pending.

A councilmember then asked the question that gets to the heart of the landowners' complaint: how can a petition drag someone into an annexation against their will? Marcus explained that state law requires signatures from owners of at least half the land area and a third of its value. Here, he said, Red Cedar Corporation signed alone and owns roughly a third of the area, and none of the other owners, most of them tied to the Holbrook family, signed. That gap is why he considers the petition invalid. He also confirmed the uncomfortable half of the answer: had those thresholds been met, state law would let a petition pull unwilling landowners in. Asked why those parcels were included at all, he described a corridor of parcels sitting between Cedar Fort and Eagle Mountain, with the Red Cedar land just north of it and the Holbrook parcels to the south, and said he could not speak to the petitioner's motives.

## Notable moments

- **A meeting called on a day's notice.** Marcus said the city became aware of the petition "yesterday," which explains both the special session and Clark's promise that it would be "short and sweet."
- **Staff corrected staff on the record.** Steve's reading of the code, that Cedar Fort can hold its September 15 hearing and deny the petition but cannot approve it, prompted Marcus to revise his own answer: "Yes, I mean, that's a little bit more, I guess, precise."
- **The mayor stayed online and stayed out of the chair.** Clark noted that Gray and Reeves were both listening in and said she would keep running the meeting "unless the mayor tells me otherwise." He did not.
- **The chair forgot a step.** After the roll call, a councilmember reminded Clark, "You need to announce that it's been, uh, what the vote was," and she went back and stated the 5-0 result. She also blamed a stumble over names on the missing nameplates: "I had a real hard time because we don't have our name plaques up."
- **No one from the public spoke,** including the landowners whose objections Marcus described to the council secondhand.

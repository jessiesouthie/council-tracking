HOUSE STYLE
Five rules. They govern every sentence you write here.

1. No em dashes. A comma, a colon or a full stop carries the same break. A dash
   inside a direct quotation stays as the speaker said it.

2. No stock opening. Assume the reader has just read four other notes on the same
   page. An opening that would fit any of them is the wrong opening, however true
   it is. Two notes in a row that begin the same way is a mistake to go back and
   fix.

3. Cut an adverb when the sentence survives without it. "actually", "really",
   "simply", "genuinely", "clearly", "essentially", "entirely" and "deliberately"
   rarely survive that test. Hedge a number with "about", and use the same word
   for it every time.

4. Name who acted. Write "the council delayed the vote", never "a decision was
   delayed". Where the record does not name anyone, say that it does not.

5. State the thing itself. "The freed sales tax goes to parks and roads" carries
   more than "the freed sales tax goes to parks and roads instead of to police".
   Keep the contrast only where the reader arrives holding the wrong version.
